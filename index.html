From photo to answer in three steps

The reading happens on your phone. The answers come from a database of regulatory facts that the app keeps up to date, never from a guess.

1

Photograph the list

Scan the label, choose a photo, or type the ingredients. You can correct the text that was read before it is checked.

2

Every ingredient is looked up

Names, E numbers and spelling variants are matched to official records for each place you chose.

3

Read each place's answer

Each answer carries its reason, its source and its date. Places are shown side by side, never merged into one.

Honest answers, in plain words

Eight states, each with a fixed meaning. The colour is not a verdict; the words are.

FlaggedA source lists the ingredient as not authorised or prohibited for food in that place.
Flagged (future)Still legal today, with a revocation that takes effect on a stated date.
AdvisoryA source has the ingredient under review. This is not a ban.
Limited useListed with conditions, or only provisionally.
No flags foundNothing to flag in the loaded sources for that place. Absence of a flag is not a safety finding.
Couldn't verifyThe app tried and could not decide: an unknown name, or no record for that place.
Named as a groupThe label gave a class word such as "flavourings" or "spices" and no substance.
Plain foodAn ordinary food such as water, sugar or wheat flour, with nothing regulatory to check.

Allergens the label names

The allergens printed on the label, in the ingredients or in a "Contains" or "May contain" line, are named for the US and the EU. Tick yours in your profile and the rows that carry them are marked. It reports what the label says; it never says an allergen is absent.

Your History, on every phone

Scans are saved with their photo and place. Sign in with your email or with Google on a new phone and your History, allergens and settings are there. Removing something only takes it out of your view.

Where the data comes from

Free public sources, with the publisher and the retrieval date shown for each answer. The data is refreshed daily when a source changes.

Regulators

  • US Food and Drug Administration: food additive and colour additive listings, post-market reviews.
  • EU Regulation (EC) No 1333/2008 and Commission acts.
  • Great Britain: the Food Standards Agency register.
  • Health Canada: the lists of food additives.

Reference data

  • Open Food Facts additives and ingredient taxonomy (Open Database Licence).
  • Public advisory events from EFSA and the European Commission.

What Snack Lore does not do. It is not a safety check, an allergy test or a certification, and it says nothing about religious or dietary status. "No flags found" means the loaded sources have nothing to flag, nothing more. Always read the printed label yourself, especially for an allergy.
